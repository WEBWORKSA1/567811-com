What is an 8-count (and why “5, 6, 7, 8”)?

How dancers count music, why choreographers say 5-6-7-8, and how to find the 1 in any song.

Updated 2026-10-08 · 567811 Editorial

In this guide
  1. The short answer
  2. Why teachers say “5, 6, 7, 8”
  3. How to find the 1
  4. “And” counts and half-time
  5. Practise drill

The short answer

Most popular music is in 4/4 time — four beats per bar. Dancers group two bars into a phrase of eight beats: the 8-count. Choreography is taught, written and rehearsed in these units.

Why teachers say “5, 6, 7, 8”

Beats 5–8 are the second half of a phrase. Calling them out loud tells everyone exactly where the next phrase begins, so the whole room starts together on 1. It's a count-in — the dance version of a drummer clicking sticks.

How to find the 1

  1. Listen for the strongest beat — often a kick drum or a big change.
  2. Count 1–8 from there; a new section (verse, chorus, drop) usually starts on a 1.
  3. Check with our Count-In Metronome: tap the BPM, then count along with accents on 1 and 5.

“And” counts and half-time

Between beats sit the “&” counts (1 & 2 & …) for quick footwork. Fast genres like salsa are often counted in slow 8s so dancers aren't counting at 200 BPM.

Practise drill

Set the metronome 20 BPM below the song's tempo, drill one 8-count until it's clean three times, then add 5 BPM. Write each count in the 8-Count Choreo Sheet.
